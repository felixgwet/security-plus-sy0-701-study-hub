## 1. You Finished the Course — What's Next?

- The final lecture is a send-off: congratulations, and a pointer to **continuing your certification journey** rather than stopping at Security+.
- The instructor's ecosystem (Dion Training) offers follow-on courses — the useful takeaway is the **certification roadmap**, not the vendor.

## 2. The CompTIA Certification Ladder (What Comes After Security+)

Think of Security+ as the **core baseline security certification**. Natural next steps:

- **CySA+ (Cybersecurity Analyst)** — defense-side: detection, analysis, response, SIEM work. The most direct continuation of Security+ skills.
- **PenTest+** — offense-side: authorized penetration testing, exploitation, reporting. Pairs with the attack knowledge from this course.
- **CASP+ / SecurityX (Advanced Security Practitioner)** — the advanced, vendor-neutral hands-on pinnacle; intended for experienced practitioners, not entry-level.
- Foundational certs (if you skipped them): **A+** (hardware/helpdesk) and **Network+** (networking) fill gaps that make Security+ material easier in hindsight.

## 3. Adjacent Tracks Worth Knowing

- **Linux (LPI Linux Essentials / LPIC)** — most security tooling runs on Linux; employers expect comfort with it.
- **ITIL 4** — service management; useful if you aim at security governance/GRC roles where process matters as much as technology.
- **PRINCE2** — project management; helpful for leading security projects and implementations.

## 4. Continuing Education: Keeping Security+ Alive

- CompTIA certifications are valid for **3 years**, renewed by earning **CEUs (Continuing Education Units)** through activities like:
  - Passing a higher-level CompTIA cert (e.g., CySA+ renews Security+ automatically).
  - Attending approved training, webinars, and conferences.
  - Publishing/teaching security content, participating in industry activities.
- Plan your CE path **before** the renewal deadline sneaks up — earning the next cert is usually the highest-value renewal move.

## 5. Turning the Certification into a Career

- Security+ is the **DoD 8570/8140 baseline** and appears in the majority of entry-level cybersecurity job postings — it gets interviews; experience gets offers.
- Typical first roles: **SOC analyst, security analyst, IT auditor, junior pen tester, GRC analyst**.
- Keep the momentum: build a home lab, participate in CTFs, follow security news (this course's threat-actor and incident-response sections are your daily reading foundation).

---

## Key Takeaways

1. Security+ is the **baseline**, not the destination — CySA+ (defense) or PenTest+ (offense) are the natural next rungs.
2. Linux and ITIL/PRINCE2 fill the technical and process gaps around the security core.
3. Your cert expires in **3 years** — CEUs or a higher cert keeps it valid; plan renewal early.
4. Certifications open doors; **labs, CTFs, and current-events knowledge** turn them into job offers.
